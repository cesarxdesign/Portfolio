The situation

A live product that couldn't be sold.

When I joined, Cable had exactly one "client" — a friend of the CEO, not paying, there as a design partner feeding us real data and feedback. The product itself was an engineering-and-client-spec build with no designer on it. It worked. It just couldn't be sold.

And this is AML and financial-crime compliance — a notoriously hard space to launch in, where a young vendor is asking institutions to trust it with their regulatory exposure. Cable was the only startup with a live product. That was the opening.

The bet

Build a product, not one client's bespoke tool.

The key call was to stop building to a single client's spec and design a coherent product any compliance team could adopt — moving Cable from reactive, client-driven builds to a systematic, UX-led experience through structured research.

That reframe started with seeing the first client for what it was: not a customer, but a design partner — a source of truth to build the real product against.
