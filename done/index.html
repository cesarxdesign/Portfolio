Co-founder. Product, design, currently building it. A fitness app on iOS, three versions. 2018-20, 2024. London, now Lisbon.

concept

Done, now what?

It started as a garage project, minus the garage. A personal trainer, two engineers, and myself. I was acting PM and PO: what we built and who for, bridging that between engineers and trainer, and keeping the tickets flowing. Which sometimes meant telling the designer he was working late again. Yup, same guy.

The seed was simple, something we've all experienced. At the gym, phone in hand, with whatever gym buddy app, just doing too much when all anyone needs is a lot less. That was the foundation we all agreed on. And I pictured it instantly: a giant button to tell the app I was done, and then it'd tell me what's next.

craft

DONE.

DONE is enormous so you cannot miss it. Sweaty hands, shaking hands, an elbow when neither hand is free. Nothing else in the UI sits close enough for a mishit. The video follows the same logic: put the phone down, get under the bar, and you can still read correct form with sweat dripping in your eyes.

Load input was harder: I wanted one control for weight and reps. A small muscle exercise might move half a kilo between sets. A squat or a deadlift can swing fifty kilos. A stepper is quick on small changes, slow on big ones. Direct input handles both, but not with wet, tired hands. So, the spinning picker: enough momentum to cross fifty kilos in one flick, enough snap to land on the half.

launch

Almost done.

It was live for about two months. About a thousand downloads, and a perfect 5.0 rating on the App Store. A great start, and a frustrating end. With nowhere left to train, user growth stalled, and the team and I moved back from London to our home countries. DONE was done.

DONE never really left my head. Four years on, the apps I used still did too much, plenty of them were doing well on the App Store, and my concept still felt like it had something to offer. So in 2024 I picked it back up, and did a full redesign. As an exercise, revisiting the concept, and testing how well it stood. With no other real constraints, I gave myself a deadline: five days. It took me six. But I liked it.

now

Not done.

AI almost gave it a second breath. With the old team gone, a developer friend, already leveraging AI to do more with less, offered to help ship the new version. Now AI is giving it its third breath.

The redesign got me reacquainted. And my recent work with AI has been leveraging my dormant engineering skills, widening my range, enabling me to build what I once could only design. So I figured I'd ship it myself. Tuning old designs, incorporating new concepts, and making the experience sharper, I even gave myself a real deadline.

Live on the App Store by September 2026. Tokens allowing.
